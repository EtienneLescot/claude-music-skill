# March

Sounds like: John Philip Sousa, Edward Elgar's ceremonial marches, film marches for armies and parades. For ceremonies, parades, sports, a confident brand.

| | |
|---|---|
| Tempo, metre | 2/4 or 4/4 at 110 to 120; 6/8 for a lilting march |
| Key, mode | major, brass-friendly keys (Bb, Eb, F); the trio in the subdominant key |
| Harmony | `I IV V I`, secondary dominants for drive, a strong `V I` at every 8 bars |
| Melody | dotted rhythms, repeated-note fanfares, a lyrical trio melody in long notes |
| Rhythm | bass on 1 and 3 (or 1 in 2/4), off-beat chords, snare patterns and rolls into phrases, cymbal and bass drum together on strong beats |
| Orchestra | trumpets and horns on the main strain, trombones and tuba on the bass and off-beats, strings and flute on the trio melody and a high obbligato, snare, bass drum, cymbals |
| Form | introduction (4 bars), first strain (16 bars, repeated), second strain (16), trio in the subdominant (lyrical, softer), break strain (dramatic), trio again in full tutti, a final "stinger" chord |
| Signature devices | the snare roll into a strain, the trio's change of key and mood, a high flute obbligato over the final trio, the stinger |
| Pitfalls | a slow or hesitant tempo; no contrast in the trio; percussion louder than the melody |
| In this studio | `trumpets`, `horns`, `trombones`, `tuba` short and long; `violins`, `flute` for the trio and obbligato; `snare`, `bass drum`, `cymbals`, `timpani` |
