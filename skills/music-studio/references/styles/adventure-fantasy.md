# Adventure, fantasy

Sounds like: John Powell's dragon films, Howard Shore's Shire, James Horner's Celtic scores, Joe Hisaishi. For journeys, flying, wide landscapes, games with a map.

| | |
|---|---|
| Tempo, metre | 6/8 at 100 to 140 (dotted quarter) for a jig-like lilt, or 4/4 at 96 to 120 |
| Key, mode | major, Mixolydian (the flat 7th) or Dorian for a folk colour |
| Harmony | `I bVII IV I`, `I V vi IV`, `I bVII I` over a drone; pedal points under the opening |
| Melody | singable, dotted rhythms, a leap of a fifth or sixth up then steps down; folk-like ornaments implied by short grace-like notes; a high point that soars |
| Rhythm | a lilting 6/8 or a running 8th ostinato; snare or tenor drum patterns for marching parts |
| Orchestra | flute for the folk voice, harp for the plucked texture, strings in octaves, horns for the heroic restatement, choir for the vista |
| Form | small statement (flute and harp), development (strings take the melody, counter-melody in horns), flight (tutti, melody in octaves), a quiet return |
| Signature devices | the melody handed from a solo instrument to the full orchestra; a sudden third-relation shift for a new vista; open fifths and drones |
| Pitfalls | the same orchestration for every statement; harmony that never leaves I-IV-V; tempo too slow for a sense of motion |
| In this studio | `flute`, `harp` short for the intro; `violins`, `celli` long for the melody; `horns` long; `tenor drum`, `snare` for a march; `choir` sopranos and altos on "a" for the vista |
