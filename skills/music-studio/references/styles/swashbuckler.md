# Swashbuckler, pirate

Sounds like: Klaus Badelt and Hans Zimmer's pirate scores, Erich Wolfgang Korngold's sea adventures. For pirates, chases, sword fights, sea voyages.

| | |
|---|---|
| Tempo, metre | 6/8 at 100 to 140 (dotted quarter), galloping |
| Key, mode | minor (D minor is the classic), harmonic minor for the raised 7th at cadences |
| Harmony | `i i bVI bVI bVII bVII V V` (two bars per chord), `i iv V`, a stop on V before the climax |
| Melody | a theme opening with a rising fifth, dotted-quarter and quarter-eighth rhythms, a question on the dominant and an answer climbing to the high tonic |
| Rhythm | the gallop: spiccato strings on all six eighths, accented on 1 and 4; low brass hits on 1 and 4 |
| Orchestra | horns on the theme, trombones an octave below, strings galloping, trumpets and high violins for the climax, anvil and big drum, male choir on held power chords |
| Form | a rolling intro (timpani roll, gallop starts), theme in horns, theme in full tutti with choir, a breakdown with tremolo, a stop on the dominant, the final statement, short brass hits to end |
| Signature devices | the gallop, anvil hits on beat 4, a cymbal swell into the climax, a final series of tutti hits |
| Pitfalls | quoting "He's a Pirate": change the rhythm and the leap; full triads in the middle register (it turns into a video game); a choir singing the fast theme |
| In this studio | `basses`, `celli`, `violas` short for the gallop; `horns`, `trombones`, `trumpets`, `violins` long; `tuba` short; `anvil`, `big drum`, `timpani`; `choir` tenors and basses |
