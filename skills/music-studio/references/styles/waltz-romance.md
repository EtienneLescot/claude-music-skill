# Waltz, romance

Sounds like: Tchaikovsky's ballet waltzes, Johann Strauss, Shostakovich's second waltz, period-drama scores. For a ball, a love scene, nostalgia, elegance.

| | |
|---|---|
| Tempo, metre | 3/4 at 150 to 180 (felt one in a bar), or slower and more intimate at 90 to 120 |
| Key, mode | major for elegance, minor for melancholy or a darker dance |
| Harmony | `I vi ii V`, `I I V V V V I I` (two bars per chord), secondary dominants (`V/V`, `V/vi`) for sweep; the minor iv for a sigh |
| Melody | long phrases of 8 bars, an appoggiatura on the downbeat resolving down, a rising sixth at the phrase's emotional peak |
| Rhythm | oom-pah-pah: bass on beat 1, chords on beats 2 and 3, the melody floating above |
| Orchestra | violins on the melody, violas and violins 2 on the "pah-pah", celli and basses on the "oom", horns on sustained harmony, flute doubling the melody an octave up, harp arpeggios |
| Form | a short introduction, waltz A (8 + 8 bars), waltz B in the dominant or the relative key, A again with fuller orchestration, a coda with a ritardando feel and a final chord |
| Signature devices | appoggiaturas, hemiola before a cadence (two bars of 3/4 felt as three of 2/4), a held top note before the cadence |
| Pitfalls | the accompaniment too loud (the "pah-pah" must stay light); the same orchestration for every repeat; a rigid tempo with no breathing in the expression |
| In this studio | `violins` long, `violins 2`, `violas` short for the chords, `celli`, `basses` short on beat 1, `horns` long, `flute`, `harp` |
