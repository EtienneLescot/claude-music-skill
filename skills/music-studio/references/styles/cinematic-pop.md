# Cinematic pop

Pop songwriting played by an orchestra: the hook, the progression and the form of a hit, the colours of film music. Sounds like: orchestral covers of pop songs, uplifting brand and social-media music, game title themes. The best choice for a short, catchy piece meant to be shared.

| | |
|---|---|
| Tempo, metre | 4/4 at 90 to 128 |
| Key, mode | major (D, E, F, G sit well for strings and brass) |
| Harmony | the pop axis: `I V vi IV`, `vi IV I V`, `I vi IV V`; one chord per bar; the answer phrase cadences `IV V` into `I` |
| Melody | a hook of one or two bars repeated with a different ending (question, answer); mostly steps with one leap; the high point in the answer phrase |
| Rhythm | a `3+3+2` ostinato in the strings, drums on 1 and the "and" of 2 (big drum) or on 2 and 4 for a pop backbeat (snare) |
| Orchestra | an intimate first statement (flute or celli with harp), the build (horns on the hook, ostinato, choir humming), the chorus (violins and trumpets in octaves, full brass, choir on syllables) |
| Form | intro (2 to 4 bars), verse (the hook small), build (8 bars), a breath (half a bar of near silence), chorus (8 bars), last chorus a whole step up (the lift), a final chord or button; 60 to 90 s for social media |
| Signature devices | the lift a step up, the breath before the chorus, the hook returning in a new register each time, a cymbal swell into each chorus |
| Pitfalls | an inaudible intro (on a phone the first seconds must carry: within about 10 LUFS of the chorus); a chorus no bigger than the verse; a lift with nothing new added |
| In this studio | `harp`, `flute` for the intro; `horns` for the build; `violins`, `trumpets`, `horns` long for the chorus; `violins 2`, `violas`, `celli` short for the ostinato; `trombones`, `tuba` short on `3+3+2`; `choir` all sections; `big drum`, `timpani`, `cymbals`; `sub` in open mode |
