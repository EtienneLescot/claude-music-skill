# Heroic, fanfare

Sounds like: John Williams, Jerry Goldsmith, Alan Silvestri's superhero themes. For a hero's entrance, victory, launch, title screens.

| | |
|---|---|
| Tempo, metre | 4/4 at 100 to 130, march-like; or a broad 3/4 for a noble theme |
| Key, mode | major, bright keys for brass (Bb, Eb, F, C, D); borrowed chords for nobility |
| Harmony | `I bVI bVII I`, `I IV I bVI bVII I`, Lydian `I II I`; strong `V I` cadences at phrase ends |
| Melody | an opening leap of a fifth or an octave, a triplet or dotted fanfare figure, a long held note at the top; answer phrase climbs higher |
| Rhythm | dotted marches, triplet pickups, a snare pattern, cymbals on arrivals |
| Orchestra | trumpets and horns on the theme in octaves, trombones on harmony, strings with an energetic counter-figure, timpani on roots |
| Form | fanfare intro (2 to 4 bars), theme A, a lyrical B in the strings, theme A again in full tutti, a big final cadence |
| Signature devices | fanfare pickups, a brass chorale, the theme in augmentation at the end, a final chord with a timpani roll |
| Pitfalls | quoting famous openings (Star Wars, Superman, Jurassic Park, Indiana Jones): write your own leap and rhythm; fast string runs that samples cannot play |
| In this studio | `trumpets`, `horns` long and short; `trombones`, `tuba`; `violins`, `violas` short for the counter-figure; `snare`, `timpani`, `cymbals` |
