# Musical culture

What each tradition contributes as technique, so a request like "something Baroque" or "a Morricone feel" turns into concrete choices. Name composers for their style, never to copy a work.

## Concert music

| Period | Composers | Techniques to borrow |
|---|---|---|
| Baroque (1600-1750) | Bach, Vivaldi, Handel | sequences through the circle of fifths, a walking bass, motor rhythm (constant 8ths or 16ths), terraced dynamics (loud then soft, no crescendo), counterpoint between two equal lines |
| Classical (1750-1820) | Mozart, Haydn, early Beethoven | periods of 4 + 4 bars, clear cadences, Alberti or repeated-note accompaniment, transparent textures, contrast between a strong first theme and a lyrical second one |
| Romantic (1820-1900) | Beethoven, Tchaikovsky, Brahms, Dvořák, Wagner | long singing melodies, rich chromatic harmony, big crescendos, the leitmotif (a theme for a character, transformed by the story), brass chorales, the orchestra as one huge instrument |
| Late Romantic, early modern | Mahler, Rachmaninoff, Holst, Elgar | extreme contrast of scale, brass in full chorale, ostinato marches (Holst's 5/4), soaring string melodies in octaves over rich harmony |
| Impressionism | Debussy, Ravel | modes and whole-tone scales, parallel chords (planing), 7ths and 9ths as colours, harp and flute, blurred pedals, orchestration as light |
| 20th century | Stravinsky, Prokofiev, Shostakovich, Copland, Barber | driving irregular rhythm, polytonality, grotesque marches, open fifths and fourths for American space (Copland), slow string adagios (Barber) |
| Minimalism | Glass, Reich, Pärt, Nyman | repeated patterns that change slowly, additive rhythm, arpeggiated ostinati, tintinnabuli (a melody with a triad note moving with it), hypnotic builds |

## Film and media

| Tradition | Composers | Techniques |
|---|---|---|
| Golden age and symphonic film | Korngold, Williams, Goldsmith, Horner | leitmotifs, Lydian and borrowed chords, brass fanfares, lush strings in octaves, a theme stated in full at the end credits |
| Western and Italian | Morricone | sparse textures, a single striking solo colour (whistle, voice, trumpet), long builds on a repeated pattern |
| Modern blockbuster | Zimmer, Badelt, Jablonsky, Balfe | slow harmony, ostinato strings, power chords in the low brass, big drums, choir, a simple motif hammered home |
| Trailer music | Two Steps From Hell, Audiomachine | three acts (mystery, build, climax), braams, risers and hits, Latin-like choir, a stop before the final act |
| Fantasy and adventure | Powell, Shore, Hisaishi | Celtic or folk modes, flute and fiddle colours, waltzes and jigs, big emotional swells |
| Game music | Uematsu, Soule, Wintory, Mitsuda | loopable sections, memorable short themes, folk instruments, adaptive layers (the same piece thinner or fuller) |
| Minimal and ambient film | Jóhannsson, Richter, Reznor and Ross | sustained textures, few notes, slow change, treated sound |

## Popular music

The studio does not yet play an acoustic drum kit, electric bass, guitars or a lead singer, so band genres can only be borrowed from, inside an orchestral or electronic piece. Synthesizers it has, through `surge.py` (`styles/electronic.md`). Their grammar still helps:

- **Pop**: verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge; the hook in the chorus title; the pop axis progressions; a lift a step up for the last chorus; 90 to 128 BPM.
- **Rock**: power chords (root and fifth), `I bVII IV` and `I IV V` progressions, riffs as motifs, drums on 2 and 4.
- **Blues and soul**: the 12-bar blues (`I I I I IV IV I I V IV I V`), dominant 7ths everywhere, the minor pentatonic over major chords.
- **Electronic** (`styles/electronic.md`): build and drop, a filter opening as a riser, four on the floor at 105 to 128, sidechain pumping, a 16th-note pulse that never stops.
- **Hip-hop and lo-fi**: a short looped progression (often `ii V I` or `i bVII bVI`), swing 16ths, 70 to 95 BPM.
- **Folk and Celtic**: drones, Mixolydian and Dorian, jig (6/8) and reel (4/4) rhythms, ornaments on the melody.
- **Latin**: clave rhythms, the Andalusian cadence, guitar-like arpeggios (harp can stand in).

Borrowing works best as one element at a time: a pop progression and hook in an orchestral cue (cinematic pop), a 12-bar blues played by brass, a minimalist ostinato under a film theme.
